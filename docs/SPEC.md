# Build the prayer app others charge for

> Product spec. The original research is kept as written. **Revisions (Oct 2026)** at
> the end lists corrections and engineering findings from building the calculation
> core. Where the two disagree, the revisions win.

A free prayer time app for iOS and Android stands out on three things: **times that match the user's local mosque, an adhan that fires every time, and a guarantee that nothing leaves the phone**. Every major competitor already offers the basics (calculated times, adhan, Qibla, Ramadan times). Users complain most about ads around sacred moments, privacy, missed Android notifications, times that don't match the mosque, and paywalled features ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/muslim-pro-quran-majeed-athan-pillars-tarteel-quran-prayer-apps-ranked-2026)). The technical core of the spec is on-device calculation with the MIT-licensed Adhan library ([batoulapps/adhan](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan)), plus notification engineering built for platform limits: iOS allows only **64 pending local notifications** and custom sounds **under 30 seconds** ([Apple Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171), [UNNotificationSound](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationsound.md)), and Android 14+ denies **SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM** by default ([Android](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)). The privacy-by-design approach is also the cheapest legally. Using a prayer app implies religion, which is sensitive data under PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25 and GDPR Art. 9, so collecting nothing avoids consent flows and impact assessments. A serverless, offline app costs about **USD 99/year (Apple) plus Google's one-time fee** to run, and an optional IAP tip jar can cover that. For an Ontario solo developer, the item most likely to delay launch is Google Play's **12-tester, 14-day closed test**. Recruit testers early.

## MVP covers seven table stakes and three free differentiators

The competitive landscape splits into ad-supported "super-apps" (Muslim Pro, Athan, Muslim & Quran, Prayer Now), which track users for advertising, and focused apps. Pillars is ad-free, but **Apple Watch is Premium at $49.99–$59.99/year** and a once-free countdown widget is now paid ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1559086853)). Mawaqit is a free non-profit with mosque-maintained iqamah times and 4.9 stars, but outside participating mosques it falls back to estimates ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1460522683), [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/feature/tech/420193/muslim-pro-alternatives-5-ad-free-prayer-and)). That leaves room for an app with Pillars' privacy approach, an optional mosque-times override like Mawaqit's, and no paywall at all. A Quran reader is the most expensive feature and the one already best served elsewhere, so it belongs last.

| Phase | Scope |
|---|---|
| **MVP (v1.0)** | All major calculation presets plus custom angles; Hanafi/Shafi'i Asr; automatic high-latitude rule; per-prayer ± minute offsets; method name shown next to times; adhan notifications with several reciters and per-prayer sound/silent/off; pre-prayer reminders; Qibla compass with calibration and accuracy display; home and lock-screen widgets with live countdown; Ramadan suhoor/Imsak and iftar; Hijri date with ±day offset; manual city search (offline) and approximate GPS; no account, no analytics |
| **v1.x** | Apple Watch / Wear OS complications (free); Live Activity "next prayer"; manual iqamah entry or mosque timetable import; prayer and fasting tracker (with menstruation pause, as Pillars does); tasbih; short azkar list; Hijri calendar view; more languages |
| **Later** | Quran text (Tanzil) and audio; mosque finder; travel mode; qada tracker |
| **Never** | Ads, upsells, social feeds, data partners |

## Calculation must expose method, madhab and latitude rule, because defaults cost 48 minutes

Most published conventions differ only in their Fajr and Isha twilight angles, or in using a fixed Isha interval. PrayTimes lists MWL 18°/17°, ISNA 15°/15°, Egyptian 19.5°/17.5°, Karachi 18°/18°, Umm al-Qura 18.5° with Isha 90 minutes after Maghrib (120 in Ramadan), Tehran 17.7°/14°, UOIF 12°/12° and MUIS 20°/18° ([PrayTimes](https://praytimes.org/docs/calculation)). Aladhan lists **22 methods**, including JAKIM, KEMENAG, Diyanet, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria and Lisbon ([Aladhan](https://aladhan.com/calculation-methods)). The research could not retrieve exact angles for several of these, so take them from the Adhan source and verify against official tables before shipping. **Canada has no central authority.** 15° is "the most common timing followed in Canada", yet masjids choose 12°, 15° or 18° independently. For Toronto in May, those choices put Fajr at 4:35, 4:11 or 3:47, a spread of **48 minutes** ([Mathabah](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/mathabah/177817/multiple-timetables-for-ramaḍan-dawn-and-suḥur-ending-12-15-and-18/)). Adhan, by contrast, recommends the Moonsighting Committee method for North America over ISNA, describing ISNA as giving "later Fajr and earlier Isha than most" ([adhan-swift](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-swift/blob/main/METHODS.md)). The spec should therefore ask during onboarding "which mosque or method do you follow?" and preselect a method from the country code (Umm al-Qura for Saudi Arabia, Karachi with Hanafi Asr for South Asia, ISNA or Moonsighting for Canada/US). That country-to-method mapping is a design choice, not a published standard.

The required settings go beyond the method. The **Asr shadow factor** is 1 for the majority schools and 2 for Hanafi ([PrayTimes](https://praytimes.org/docs/calculation)), and Hanafi Asr can fall 30–90 minutes later ([ConvertTime](https://converttime.com/en/blog/prayer-time-differences)). The **Moonsighting Committee** method needs seasonal Fajr/Isha functions, a choice of red or white twilight (shafaq), the 1/7-of-night rule between 55° and 60°, Maghrib = sunset + 3 min and Dhuhr = noon + 5 min ([Moonsighting.com](https://www.moonsighting.com/how-we.html)). **High-latitude handling is the biggest correctness risk**. Adhan defaults to middle-of-night and recommends seventh-of-night above 48° ([adhan-swift](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan-swift/blob/main/METHODS.md)). One app without such a rule showed Fajr at 1:25 and Isha at 1:24 in German summer until a fix shipped ([GTAF](https://feedback.gtaf.org/sadiq/p/unusual-prayer-times-for-fajr-and-isha-in-germany)). Ship `recommended(for:)` as the default, plus a polar fallback (nearest location or nearest date) ([adhan-js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/batoulapps/adhan-js/master/METHODS.md)). Compute in UTC and store an **IANA timezone with each location**, never a fixed offset, so DST and manual cities resolve correctly. Location precision matters little: a few km moves times by about 30 seconds, while differing defaults move them by 10–20 minutes ([ConvertTime](https://converttime.com/en/blog/prayer-time-differences)). Show iqamah separately from the adhan (start) time, because mosques hold the iqamah 10–20 minutes later and users read the gap as an app error.

The **Qibla** is the initial great-circle bearing to the Kaaba, which from North America points northeast ([IslamQA](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/126380/what-is-the-correct-qibla-for-north-america/)). Phones read magnetic north, so the app must correct for magnetic declination using platform true-heading APIs ([Qibla calc](https://ftp.unpad.ac.id/orari/library/library-islam/knowledge/qibla_calc.html)), show calibration status, and warn about metal nearby. For **Hijri dates**, use the platform's Umm al-Qura calendar (CLDR `islamic-umalqura`) with a **±2 day user offset**. Sighting-based Ramadan and Eid dates can't be computed in advance, and they differ from Umm al-Qura by 1–3 days ([CLDR](https://cldr.unicode.org/development/development-process/design-proposals/islamic-calendar-types), [van Gent](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_introduction.htm)). The Umm al-Qura Ramadan Isha rule has to follow the adjusted Hijri date. Imsak (Fajr − 10 min), Dhuha and the last third of the night are library conventions rather than sourced rulings, so make them configurable. Use **adhan-testdata** as golden vectors ([batoulapps/adhan](https://github.com/batoulapps/adhan)), and add tests for DST changeover days, offsets of +5:30 and +5:45, southern-hemisphere locations, and Oslo/Reykjavik summers.

## Notifications decide the stack: native code is unavoidable on both platforms

Reliability of the adhan is the most emotionally charged failure in competitor reviews (Athan on Android, Sajda, Salatuk on iOS) ([Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/muslim-pro-quran-majeed-athan-pillars-tarteel-quran-prayer-apps-ranked-2026), [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/salatuk/686077611)). Exact alarms, widgets, Live Activities, AlarmKit and watch complications all need native code. That means Flutter or React Native only saves effort on the main UI. React Native has the official adhan-js port, while Flutter's adhan-dart is a community port. Native Swift + Kotlin uses the official adhan-swift and adhan-java ports and has the least friction with platform features. Aladhan's free API is rate-limited per IP (about 12 req/s, which carrier NAT can exhaust) ([Islamic Network](https://community.islamic.network/d/2-is-there-a-rate-limit-on-the-apis)), so use it only for cross-checking, never as a runtime dependency.

**iOS.** The 64-notification ceiling "is a system limit and there is no way around it" ([Apple Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171)). Five adhans a day fill it in about 12 days, or about 6 days once pre-prayer reminders are added. The app must therefore refill a rolling window on every launch, on background refresh, and on timezone or significant-location change, and remind the user to open the app weekly. Notification sounds must be aiff/wav/caf **under 30 seconds**, or iOS plays the default sound ([UNNotificationSound](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationsound.md)). Ship trimmed takbir clips and play the full adhan when the user taps into the app. **AlarmKit (iOS 26+)** overrides silent mode and Focus with user authorization and an `NSAlarmKitUsageDescription` string ([AlarmKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/alarmkit/scheduling-an-alarm-with-alarmkit.md)). Its repeat schedule is fixed weekly, so prayer adhans would need one-time alarms on a rolling window. Two things are unverified: whether App Review accepts this use for a prayer app, and whether AlarmKit supports custom sound length. Treat AlarmKit as an opt-in "loud adhan" mode. Critical Alerts need an Apple entitlement intended for health and safety, so don't plan on getting it ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.usernotifications.critical-alerts.md)).

**Android.** Check `canScheduleExactAlarms()`, send the user to `ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, re-check in `onResume`, and fall back to `setWindow()`. **USE_EXACT_ALARM is reserved for alarm-clock and calendar apps** and requires a Play declaration ([Android](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms), [Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13161072)). Arm only the next prayer with `setAlarmClock` or `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`. When it fires, start a `mediaPlayback` or `shortService` (about 3 minutes) foreground service to play the full adhan. Exact-alarm triggers are an allowed exemption for starting such a service from the background ([FGS restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start), [FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). Re-arm on each fire and on `BOOT_COMPLETED`, `TIME_SET` and `TIMEZONE_CHANGED`. Note that apps targeting Android 15+ cannot start a mediaPlayback service from boot. Onboarding should also request POST_NOTIFICATIONS and battery-optimization exemption, with deep links for Xiaomi and Samsung, which DontKillMyApp ranks among the worst battery killers ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/)). Create a separate notification channel per sound, since a channel's sound can't be changed after creation.

**Surfaces.** A WidgetKit timeline can hold about six prayer-boundary entries per day with `Text(timerInterval:)` countdowns that consume no refresh budget ([WidgetKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date.md)), which avoids the stale-widget problem. Live Activities are capped at 8 hours (12 on the Lock Screen) and have no network or location access, so schedule them locally with `startDate` before each prayer rather than running an APNs server ([ActivityKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities.md)). On Android, use Glance widgets driven by the same alarm. Wear OS and watchOS complication specifics were not researched.

## Collecting nothing is both the brand and the compliance strategy

In 2020, Muslim Pro (98M+ downloads) sent granular location to X-Mode, and that data reached US military customers through defense contractors. X-Mode was not in its privacy policy ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/muslim-pro-location-data-military-xmode)). Pillars launched in direct response by computing everything on the device ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/ikrd/pillars-app)). Under OPC guidance, religious beliefs "will generally be considered sensitive" and require express consent ([OPC](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/)). Quebec Law 25 adds privacy-by-default, a named privacy officer, PIAs before transfers outside Quebec, and penalties up to CAD 10M ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/quebec-law-25/)). GDPR Art. 9 restricts processing of religious data ([GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)). The non-functional requirements follow from this: all calculation on the device; **no ad, analytics, attribution or default Crashlytics SDKs** (crash reporting opt-in only); approximate location (or manual city only); an offline city database, for example GeoNames with per-city IANA zones (the CC BY license is unverified); and a plain-language policy that names the developer as privacy contact. Open-sourcing the code makes the privacy claims verifiable. Ontario has no private-sector privacy statute. Whether PIPEDA reaches a free, revenue-less app is unresolved, but building as if it does costs nothing.

On the store side, Apple requires the **$99 USD/year** program ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/)), a privacy manifest with required-reason APIs such as UserDefaults ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files)), and manual address entry if location is declined. Apps may not require location to work (5.1.1(iv), 5.1.2(i)) ([Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). With no accounts and no tracking, account deletion and ATT don't apply, and the label can honestly read "Data Not Collected". Google requires a Data safety form and privacy-policy link even when nothing is collected, and on-device processing doesn't count as "collected" ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)). New and updated apps must **target API 36 from 31 Aug 2026** ([Android](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). New personal accounts must run a **closed test with 12+ testers for 14 consecutive days** ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en-GB)). For content, Tanzil's Quran text is CC BY 3.0, verbatim only, with credit and a link ([Tanzil](https://tanzil.net/docs/text_license)). Quran Foundation's terms forbid prepackaged databases and caching beyond 7 days ([QF](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/legal/developer-terms/)), which rules it out for offline use. No authoritative source covers adhan audio licensing, so record with a muezzin under a signed release, or use recordings with documented CC0/CC-BY provenance, and keep a license register in the repo.

**Localization and accessibility** (no sources were fetched for this area; these follow platform conventions). The app needs RTL layouts for Arabic and Urdu, mirroring navigation but not the clock or compass dial. Urdu needs a Nastaliq font with extra line height. Use locale-aware digits (Arabic-Indic, extended Persian/Urdu, Bengali) with a user override. Translate prayer names, with transliteration choices. Priority locales are English, Arabic, Urdu, Bengali, French, Turkish, Indonesian and Malay. Accessibility requirements are full VoiceOver/TalkBack labels (for example "Asr, 3:42 PM, next prayer in 25 minutes"), haptic and audio Qibla feedback, 44pt/48dp touch targets, 200% font scaling, and WCAG 2.1 AA contrast in both light and dark mode. AODA's applicability to an individual developer was not verified, so treat WCAG AA as best practice.

**Sustainability.** Apple permits IAP tips (3.1.1), at the 15% Small Business rate ([SBP](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). Only approved nonprofits may fundraise in-app, and others must collect funds outside the app (3.2.1(vi), 3.2.2(iv)) ([Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). A serverless app with a GitHub Pages policy site costs about USD 99/year plus an optional domain, so an ungated "sadaqah jariyah" tip jar through IAP and Play Billing can cover costs. Registering a nonprofit later could unlock Apple's fee waiver. Three things are unresolved: Play's tip policy, the Google fee amount (believed to be $25 USD), and whether tips count as taxable income for a Canadian individual (check CRA rules).

## Conclusion

The scope that matters most is narrow and close to infrastructure: a correct method and latitude rule, a timezone stored with each location, and a notification scheduler that survives iOS slot limits and Android battery killers. That is where competitors lose user trust, and where a solo developer can beat funded teams. The Muslim Pro episode turned privacy into a feature users are actively looking for, and for a developer in Canada, collecting nothing is also the simplest way to comply with sensitive-data rules. Three open items need checking before committing to the design: whether Apple accepts AlarmKit for adhans, whether Play accepts USE_EXACT_ALARM for this use, and the exact parameters of the regional methods that couldn't be retrieved. All three can be settled with small prototypes or a submission test. The UI framework choice doesn't need deep research, because native modules are needed either way.

## Revisions (Oct 2026)

### Facts that changed or were settled

- **Target API 36 is already required.** The 31 Aug 2026 deadline has passed, so it's a current requirement, not a future gate.
- **Google Play's registration fee is USD 25, paid once.**
- **GeoNames is CC BY 4.0.** Attribution in the About screen is enough. GPS fixes still need a timezone: use the nearest GeoNames city's zone, or bundle a timezone-boundary file.
- **Stack decided: native Swift + Kotlin** (see `docs/adr/0001-native-swift-kotlin.md`).
- **"adhan-java" is now adhan-kotlin**, a Kotlin Multiplatform library published as `com.batoulapps.adhan:adhan2`. It's used on Android as a plain JVM dependency.

### Findings from building the calculation core

Each finding is covered by tests in both `android/core` and `ios/PrayerCore`.

1. **Polar days can return garbage, not an error.** Near the polar circles, adhan-kotlin 0.0.7 returned times out of order instead of failing: in Tromsø on 5 Jan, Asr came out *after* Maghrib. adhan-swift 1.5.0 has no ordering check either. The wrapper rejects any day whose times aren't at least 5 minutes apart and in order, then applies the nearest-latitude fallback.
2. **Polar fallback is "nearest latitude".** The calculator steps toward the equator in 0.5° increments until times exist, and reports `PolarFallback(latitudeUsed)` so the UI can say so. Adhan's `SunnahTimes` fails after a fallback, so the wrapper computes the middle and last third of the night itself.
3. **The date line needs a solar-date shift.** Adhan reads its date input as the solar day at that longitude. In zones far from longitude/15 hours (Kiritimati UTC+14, Apia UTC+13), passing the civil date returns the *next* day's times. The wrapper converts the civil date first.
4. **Tehran is excluded for now.** Only adhan-swift supports a Maghrib angle, so offering Tehran would make the two apps disagree. Both apps offer the same 11 presets plus custom angles.
5. **Ramadan Isha follows the adjusted Hijri date.** Umm al-Qura's +30 minutes uses the Hijri date *after* the user's ±2-day offset. This is tested on 1 Ramadan 1447 (18 Feb 2026).
6. **Toronto's spread is confirmed.** On 15 May 2026, Fajr at 12°, 15° and 18° falls at 04:37, 04:15 and 03:49, 48 minutes apart, matching the Mathabah figure.

### Corrections to the notification and surface design

- **Live Activities can't be scheduled locally.** An app generally starts one only while it's in the foreground, or by push-to-start, which needs a server. Prototype before committing to it. Otherwise start one only while the app is open, or drop the feature.
- **Android: arm 48 hours of prayers, not just the next one.** If one re-arm fails, every later adhan is lost without warning. The whole set is re-synced each time an alarm fires. Android allows about 500 alarms per app. Built: see `docs/NOTIFICATIONS.md`.
- **iOS: the 64th slot is a safety net.** The last pending notification, 24 hours before the window ends, says "Open the app to keep adhans scheduled", which covers users who force-quit the app. Built: see `docs/NOTIFICATIONS.md`.
- **iOS travel mode: don't use significant-location change.** It needs "Always" location, which contradicts the privacy stance. Refresh on app open, and treat a timezone change as the background trigger.
- **Qibla without location permission.** iOS `trueHeading` needs location services. For a manually chosen city, compute declination on-device from the city's coordinates using the World Magnetic Model. Android's `GeomagneticField` already does this.
- **Opt-in crash reporting is still "collection".** Once a user enables it, the Data safety form and Apple's privacy label must declare it. Prefer no crash SDK, using App Store Connect crash logs and Play Console vitals instead.
- **Privacy manifest entry.** Our own `UserDefaults` use needs required-reason `CA92.1`, even with no SDKs.
- **Timezone-safe triggers.** On iOS, build notification triggers from absolute instants, or from `DateComponents` with the place's `timeZone` set. Never use device-local wall-clock components.

### Still open

1. Does App Review accept AlarmKit for adhans, and does AlarmKit support custom sound length?
2. Does Play accept USE_EXACT_ALARM for this use?
3. Can a Live Activity be started without a server?
4. Official parameters for JAKIM, KEMENAG, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Lisbon and UOIF. Until verified, users can enter these as custom angles.
5. Do java.time's `HijrahChronology` and Apple's `islamicUmmAlQura` agree on every date? Both are tested on Ramadan 1447. Add a full-year cross-check.
